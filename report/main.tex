\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
I solve the steady variable-diffusion problem \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)=f\) on the unit square (Problem Pack Direction~3) by a conservative face-averaged five-point finite-difference method, assembled in COO and solved as CSR. Dirichlet data are eliminated into the right-hand side; boundary nodes are never unknowns. On the smooth manufactured benchmark \(a=1+x\), \(u^*=\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\), the discrete \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) errors both fit order \(2.00\) over \(n=15,31,63,127\), and \(\|A-A^\top\|_{\max}/\|A\|_{\max}=0\). On the layered jump \(a=1\,|\,10\) at \(x=1/2\), a harmonic (resistance) face average reproduces the piecewise-linear exact solution at the nodes to \(10^{-14}\) and keeps the interface flux at \(20/11\) to the linear-solve accuracy; an arithmetic average of the same two nodal values yields only first-order \(L^\infty\) errors and a flux jump that does not vanish under refinement. Unpreconditioned CG, stopped on the residual, matches the direct \(L^2\) error once the relative residual is \(10^{-4}\); at \(n=31\) there is no runtime crossover with \texttt{spsolve}. The main limitation is that the scheme is first-order at an unresolved jump if the face coefficient is chosen arithmetically, and that CG iteration counts grow with both \(1/h\) and the contrast \(a_R/a_L\).
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} elliptic BVP, variable diffusion, five-point stencil, harmonic average, method of manufactured solutions, sparse direct / CG

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\subsection{Problem Background}
Steady heat conduction in a heterogeneous medium---a layered composite, a soil profile, a tissue boundary---is the model problem in which the conductivity \(a(x,y)\) is not constant and may jump~\cite{problemPack,hackbusch2017}. The physically correct operator is the divergence form \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)\), because that is the statement that the normal flux \(a\partial_n u\) is continuous across an interface. A pointwise expansion \(a\Delta u+\nabla a\cdot\nabla u\) is the wrong starting point once \(a\) is discontinuous: it loses conservation, symmetry, and the M-matrix sign pattern.

I chose Direction~3 rather than a curved-disk Poisson problem because the unit square lets the five-point machinery from Tutorial~5 stay intact, while the jump forces a genuine modelling choice (arithmetic versus harmonic face coefficients) that a smooth manufactured Poisson problem never sees. This report is a solo submission: I derived the stencil, wrote the solvers, ran the experiments, and typeset the text.

\subsection{Mathematical Model}
On \(\Omega=(0,1)^2\) with Dirichlet data on the whole boundary,
\begin{equation}
-\nabla\cdot\bigl(a(x,y)\nabla u\bigr)=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad
u=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with \(0<a_{\min}\le a(x,y)\le a_{\max}<\infty\). The sign convention is the one that makes \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)\) a positive operator: the discrete matrix will have a positive diagonal.

\paragraph{Smooth manufactured benchmark (bank P4).}
\[
a(x,y)=1+x,\qquad
u^*(x,y)=\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y),\qquad
g=0,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
f=2\pi^2(1+x)\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)-\pi\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y).
\label{eq:f4}
\end{equation}
The extra \(-\pi\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\) term is \(\nabla a\cdot\nabla u^*\); it is the reason a constant-coefficient five-point stencil is not enough.

\paragraph{Layered-interface benchmark.}
\[
a(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x<1/2,\\10,&x>1/2,\end{cases}
\qquad f=0,
\]
and \(g=u^*\) on \(\partial\Omega\), where the exact one-dimensional profile
\begin{equation}
u^*(x)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{20}{11}\,x,&0\le x\le 1/2,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{2}{11}\,x+\dfrac{9}{11},&1/2\le x\le 1,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ulayered}
\end{equation}
is independent of \(y\). It is continuous, \(u^*(0)=0\), \(u^*(1)=1\), the derivative jumps, and the flux \(a u_x^*=20/11\) is continuous. Well-posedness follows from uniform ellipticity and a nonempty Dirichlet boundary; no compatibility condition is required.

Expected signs: \(u^*\ge 0\) on the layered problem, with extrema \(0\) and \(1\) attained on the boundary (maximum principle). The smooth P4 solution is a positive first eigenfunction of \(-\Delta\) modulated by \(a=1+x\), so \(u^*>0\) in \(\Omega\) and \(u^*=0\) on \(\partial\Omega\).

\section{Discretisation and Analysis}
\label{sec:disc}
\subsection{Discrete Scheme}
I use a uniform nodal grid with \(n\) interior nodes per direction,
\begin{equation}
h=\frac{1}{n+1},\qquad
x_i=(i+1)h,\qquad
y_j=(j+1)h,\qquad
i,j=0,\ldots,n-1.
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
The unknown vector is indexed by \(k=i n+j\) (\texttt{meshgrid} \texttt{indexing='ij'}, axis \(0\) is \(x\)). Boundary nodes are not unknowns.

A conservative finite-volume / face-centred reading of \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)\) on the dual cell of width \(h\) gives the five-point divergence-form stencil~\cite{leveque2007,problemPack}
\begin{equation}
(A_h U)_{ij}
=\frac{1}{h^2}\Bigl[
a_E(U_{ij}-U_{i+1,j})
+a_W(U_{ij}-U_{i-1,j})
+a_N(U_{ij}-U_{i,j+1})
+a_S(U_{ij}-U_{i,j-1})
\Bigr].
\label{eq:stencil}
\end{equation}
When \(a\equiv 1\) this is the classical five-point Laplacian
\[
(-\Delta_h u)_{ij}=\frac{4u_{ij}-u_W-u_E-u_S-u_N}{h^2}.
\]
The four face coefficients are shared: the east coefficient of node \((i,j)\) is the west coefficient of node \((i+1,j)\). That sharing, not the particular average, is what makes \(A_h\) symmetric after Dirichlet elimination.

For smooth \(a\) I evaluate the coefficient at the half-point,
\(a_E=a(x_i+h/2,y_j)\). For \(a=1+x\) this coincides with the arithmetic nodal midpoint and is second-order. At a material interface I use the resistance (harmonic) average of the two nodal values,
\begin{equation}
a_E=\frac{2 a_L a_R}{a_L+a_R},
\label{eq:harm}
\end{equation}
and, as a deliberate comparison, the arithmetic mean \((a_L+a_R)/2\). When the interface bisects a grid edge, \eqref{eq:harm} is exact for a piecewise-constant \(a\). I therefore take odd interval counts \(N=n+1\in\{31,63,127,255\}\) on the layered test, so \(x=1/2\) lies midway between two interior columns~\cite{problemPack}.

\subsection{Consistency and Truncation Error}
Taylor expansion of \eqref{eq:stencil} for smooth \(a\) and smooth \(u\) reproduces \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)\) with leading term \(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\) (the same \(u_{xxxx}\) and \(u_{yyyy}\) pieces as the five-point Laplacian, plus smooth lower-order terms from the variation of \(a\)). The assumption is \(u\in C^4\) and \(a\in C^2\). It is false across the jump: the layered \(u^*\) is only Lipschitz, and I do not claim second order for an arithmetic face coefficient there.

I measure the truncation residual \(\tau_h=b-A_h u^*\) in the discrete \(L^2\) norm \(h\|\tau_h\|_2\). On P4 this quantity drops by four under each mesh doubling (Table~\ref{tab:trunc}), consistent with \(\mathcal{O}(h^2)\) pointwise truncation.

\subsection{Boundary Treatment}
Every neighbour that lands on \(\partial\Omega\) is removed from the unknown list. If the stencil at interior index \(k\) has a Dirichlet neighbour with value \(g_{\mathrm{edge}}\), the term \(a_{\mathrm{face}}(U_k-g_{\mathrm{edge}})/h^2\) contributes
\begin{equation}
b_k \mathrel{+}= \frac{a_{\mathrm{face}}\,g_{\mathrm{edge}}}{h^2}
\label{eq:dirichlet}
\end{equation}
to the right-hand side, with the same sign as moving \(-(-a_{\mathrm{face}} g_{\mathrm{edge}})/h^2\) across. For P5 (\(u^*=e^x\sin(\pi y)\), \(a=1\)) the west column \(i=0\) is the row set \(k=0,\ldots,n-1\); an example is
\[
b[0]\mathrel{+}=\frac{e^{0}\sin(\pi y_0)}{h^2}.
\]
Replacing a Dirichlet row by \(e_k\) while leaving the corresponding column untouched would destroy symmetry~\cite{problemPack}. I never do that: boundary nodes are absent from \(A_h\).

This project is pure Dirichlet. There is no Neumann/Robin closure and therefore no one-sided versus ghost-point comparison.

\subsection{Discrete Structure}
After Dirichlet elimination, every off-diagonal entry of \(A_h\) is non-positive and the row sums on interior stencils that do not touch the boundary are zero; boundary-adjacent rows are strictly diagonally dominant because a positive \(a_{\mathrm{face}}/h^2\) has been moved to \(b\). With positive shared face coefficients, \(A_h\) is a symmetric M-matrix, hence SPD, and the discrete maximum principle holds: a discrete solution of \(A_h U=0\) with Dirichlet data in \([m,M]\) remains in \([m,M]\).

I check three numerical corroborations, none of which replaces the derivation: \(\|A-A^\top\|_{\max}/\|A\|_{\max}=0\) on every assembled matrix in this report; the smallest algebraic eigenvalue of the P4 matrix at \(n=15\) is \(\lambda_{\min}=28.61>0\); the layered numerical solution stays in \([0,1]\). Conditioning is a separate fact: \(\kappa_2(A_h)\) on P4 grows like \(h^{-2}\) (\(126.5\), \(529.7\), \(2.18\times 10^3\) at \(n=15,31,63\)), as expected for a second-order elliptic operator.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
\subsection{Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Assembly of \(A_h U=b\) and the direct solve}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require interior count \(n\), coefficient \(a\), data \(f,g\), face rule (half-point / harmonic / arithmetic)
\Ensure \(u_h\), residual \(\|b-A_h u_h\|_\infty\)
\State \(h\leftarrow 1/(n+1)\); index \(k=in+j\)
\State \(b\leftarrow f\) sampled at interior nodes
\For{each interior node \((i,j)\)}
  \For{each of the four faces}
    \State compute a shared \(a_{\mathrm{face}}>0\)
    \If{the neighbour is interior}
      \State add \(a_{\mathrm{face}}/h^2\) to the diagonal and \(-a_{\mathrm{face}}/h^2\) to that column
    \Else
      \State add \(a_{\mathrm{face}}/h^2\) to the diagonal and \(a_{\mathrm{face}} g_{\mathrm{edge}}/h^2\) to \(b_k\)
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State convert COO triplets to CSR; \(u_h\leftarrow\texttt{spsolve}(A,b)\)
\State report \(\|b-A u_h\|_\infty\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Key Design Decisions}
I assemble in COO and convert to CSR once, never through a dense matrix. The physics (\(a\), \(f\), \(g\)) is isolated in \texttt{model.py} so the same assembler runs P4, P5 and the layered jump. The default solve is sparse direct: at the meshes in this report (\(n\le 254\), \(N_{\mathrm{unk}}\le 6.5\times 10^4\), five nonzeros per row) a banded factorisation is cheaper than unpreconditioned CG, which I still run as a residual-stopped iterative check and as a contrast-sensitivity probe. The cost measure is named before it is quoted: for CG it is the iteration count to a stated relative residual; for the direct solver it is one sparse factorisation, with wall time reported only as a secondary observation. I do not count assembly, eigenvalue estimates, or plotting.

\subsection{Separating Algebraic and Discretisation Error}
\texttt{spsolve} has no tolerance parameter. I treat its solution as the exact discrete solution and report the backward residual. On P4 at \(n=31\), \(\|b-Au_h\|_\infty=3.00\times 10^{-12}\) while the discrete \(L^2\) error is \(3.99\times 10^{-4}\). A crude forward-error sketch \(\|e\|_2\lesssim \|r\|_2/\lambda_{\min}\) with \(\lambda_{\min}\approx 28.7\) puts the algebraic error near \(10^{-13}\), four orders below the discretisation error.

Unpreconditioned CG on the same matrix, stopped on the relative residual \(\|r_k\|_\infty/\|r_0\|_\infty\), is the second demonstration (Figure~\ref{fig:cg}). At relative tolerance \(10^{-2}\) the discrete \(L^2\) error is still \(4.33\times 10^{-4}\); from \(10^{-4}\) onwards it has locked onto the direct value \(3.988\times 10^{-4}\) (Table~\ref{tab:cg}). Tightening further does not change the reported discretisation error, so the fitted order in Table~\ref{tab:p4} is not a solver artefact. I stop CG on the residual, never on \(\|x^{k+1}-x^k\|\).

\subsection{Depth Comparison: Harmonic versus Arithmetic Face Coefficients}
The comparison uses the same layered problem, the same odd-\(N\) grids, the same Dirichlet data~\eqref{eq:ulayered}, and the same direct solver. The only change is the face rule at every edge, including the interface. Table~\ref{tab:layer} and Figures~\ref{fig:profile}--\ref{fig:layerorder} are the evidence.

Harmonic averaging is nodally exact: \(L^\infty\le 3\times 10^{-14}\) on all four meshes, and the numerical flux \(a_E(U_R-U_L)/h\) equals \(20/11\) to at most \(5\times 10^{-13}\). That is the pack's sanity check~\cite{problemPack}. An interface-fitted 1D P1 FEM with \(32\) elements (so \(x=1/2\) is a node, the opposite alignment of the FDM grid) reproduces the same profile to \(L^\infty=6.7\times 10^{-16}\). Two methods that share neither the stencil nor the mesh convention agree on the exact nodal values.

Arithmetic averaging is a consistent elliptic operator and still produces a symmetric M-matrix---and it is the wrong interface resistance. The flux jump stays \(\approx 0.66\) under refinement (it does not converge to zero). The \(L^\infty\) solution error is first-order (\(\approx 1.94\times 10^{-2}\) down to \(2.38\times 10^{-3}\) as \(N\) runs through \(31,63,127,255\)). A symmetric matrix alone does not prove that the interface physics is correct.

On every harmonic and arithmetic run the discrete solution stays in \([0,1]\). I read that as the M-matrix sign pattern, not as an accuracy claim: the arithmetic solution is equally monotone and equally wrong at the jump.

\subsection{Main Results: Contrast, Conditioning, Cost}
Unpreconditioned CG iteration counts on the layered harmonic matrix at \(n=30\), relative residual \(10^{-10}\), grow with the contrast \(a_R/a_L\): \(125\), \(227\), \(401\), \(615\) iterations at contrasts \(2,10,50,200\) (Figure~\ref{fig:contrast}). Coefficient contrast worsens conditioning but does not, by itself, break the discrete maximum principle. On the smooth P4 problem, \(\kappa_2(A_h)\) tracks \(h^{-2}\) as already quoted. At the meshes I actually solved, \texttt{spsolve} remains the cheaper way to reach a residual below the discretisation error; I did not observe a CG--direct crossover for \(n\le 31\).

\subsection{Limitations and Robustness}
The second-order claim is for smooth \(a\) and the half-point (or equivalent arithmetic-on-smooth) face rule. Across a jump, harmonic averaging on an aligned grid is exact for this piecewise-linear \(u^*\); that is a 1D identity, not a general theorem for rough interfaces that cut through cells at arbitrary angles. Arithmetic averaging remains an M-matrix and will still look ``stable'' on a colour plot. I would not use it as a reference closure.

I did not implement algebraic multigrid on the FDM matrix, nor a 2D interface-fitted FEM comparison beyond the single P4 oracle. Unpreconditioned CG will not be the iterative method of choice on finer 2D meshes; the Tutorial~6 AMG-CG demo is the natural next solver, not a result of this report. The scheme is two-dimensional and Cartesian; a cut-cell disk would need a different boundary row.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conc}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item I assembled a conservative face-averaged five-point scheme for \(-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)\) with Dirichlet data moved into \(b\), and I verified it on the analytic bank before the jump.
  \item On smooth P4, discrete \(L^2\) and \(L^\infty\) both fit order \(2.00\) over four meshes; \(\|A-A^\top\|_{\max}=0\) and \(\lambda_{\min}>0\). The reported rate survives a CG tolerance sweep.
  \item On the layered jump, harmonic face coefficients are nodally exact and keep the flux at \(20/11\). Arithmetic coefficients give a first-order solution error and an \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) flux jump. Symmetry is necessary and not sufficient.
  \item Cost is CG iterations or one sparse factorisation. At \(n=31\), CG and \texttt{spsolve} agree in \(L^2\) once the relative residual is \(10^{-4}\); there is no runtime crossover on this range. CG iterations grow with contrast.
\end{itemize}
